# Provenance and limits

## Sources

- **Plato**, early dialogues: *Euthyphro*, *Laches*, *Charmides*, *Meno*, *Republic* I. Primary source for the elenchus pattern: definition offered → refutation → aporia → retry. Not a pedagogy study; a literary-philosophical depiction of a method.
- **Paul, R., & Elder, L. (2006).** *Critical Thinking: Tools for Taking Charge of Your Learning and Your Life* (2nd ed.), and *The Miniature Guide to Critical Thinking Concepts and Tools*. Framework for elements of thought (including **concepts**) and intellectual standards (clarity, precision, accuracy, relevance, depth, breadth, logic, significance, fairness).
- **Ennis, R. H. (1996).** *Critical Thinking*. Prentice Hall. Also Ennis (1987), "A taxonomy of critical thinking dispositions and abilities," in Baron & Sternberg (eds.), *Teaching Thinking Skills*. Source for clarifying-meaning abilities, definitional adequacy criteria (too broad / too narrow / circular / emotive / obscure), and the stipulative-vs-reportive distinction.
- **Stevenson, C. L. (1938/1944).** "Persuasive Definitions," *Mind*. Origin of the persuasive-definition category used in Move 1.
- **Bridgman, P. W. (1927).** *The Logic of Modern Physics*. Operationalism, the ancestor of "operational definition" as used in Move 4; the skill uses the weaker measurement-theory sense, not Bridgman's strict operationalism.
- **Wittgenstein, L. (1953).** *Philosophical Investigations*, §§65–67. Family resemblance, the reason a classical definition can legitimately fail for some terms.
- **Walton, D. (2008).** *Informal Logic: A Pragmatic Approach*. Context-sensitivity of definitional disputes and the verbal/substantive distinction.

## What is evidence, and what is not

- The elenchus is documented as a **textual pattern in Plato**, not as an experimentally validated training method. Claims that "Socratic questioning improves critical thinking" rest on heterogeneous classroom studies with weak designs; treat the method here as a **structured heuristic**, not a proven booster.
- Paul–Elder is a widely used **framework and rubric**, not a body of experimental findings. Its value in this skill is organizational: it supplies the vocabulary (concepts, clarity, precision, fairness) that makes the chain teachable and checkable.
- Ennis's definitional criteria are **normative criteria** refined in logic and philosophy of language. They are reliable as checks; they are not effect sizes.
- Operationalization has a real cost: any indicator is narrower than the concept, and optimizing it invites Goodhart effects. The skill therefore reports the indicator *and* the guardrail rather than treating the number as the concept.
- No claim is made that this skill improves decision outcomes in general. The honest claim is narrower: it prevents a specific failure — **arguing at length about a term whose referent was never fixed** — by producing a definition that can be tested.

## Known limits

- **Adversarial drift.** Elenchus can read as interrogation or "gotcha". The guardrails (probe budget, candidate revisions, respecting stipulation) are load-bearing, not decoration.
- **Aporia is not an answer.** For practical work the chain must reach a revision and an operational test; stopping at "your definition is broken" is a failure of this skill.
- **Tacit knowledge.** Some terms (craft judgment, clinical intuition) resist full operationalization. Forcing a metric can distort the practice; prefer a threshold plus a human-coded judgment and say so.
- **Stipulated terms.** In law, contracts, and standards, the definition is authoritative by decision. Test usefulness and edge cases, not truth.
- **Family resemblance.** For prototype-structured terms, demanding necessary and sufficient conditions produces false refutations. Switch to purpose-relative criteria or a graded threshold.
- **Facilitator effect.** The quality of the probes depends on domain knowledge; a generic probe set (as generated by the helper script) is a checklist, not a substitute for reading the actual cases.
- **False precision.** A crisp operational definition of a vague concept can launder a value judgment as a measurement. Name the value choice when setting thresholds.
