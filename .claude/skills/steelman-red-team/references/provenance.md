# Provenance and Honesty Notes

This skill is a **structured heuristic**, not a validated intervention. It packages procedures with a long philosophical and methodological pedigree; it does not claim an effect size for "steelmanning training."

## Intellectual roots

### Paul & Elder — fair-mindedness and intellectual empathy

*Critical Thinking: Tools for Taking Charge of Your Professional and Personal Life* (2006), and the Paul–Elder framework's intellectual traits.

- Two traits do the work here: **intellectual empathy** (reconstructing viewpoints you disagree with accurately, to understand rather than to defeat) and **fair-mindedness** (applying the same evidential standards to your side as to the rival's).
- The framework's intellectual standards — clarity, accuracy, precision, relevance, depth, breadth, logic, significance, fairness — are the checklist behind the "guardrails" in this skill.
- Paul–Elder supplies the *disposition* (wanting the strongest rival case) and the *standard* (symmetry of scrutiny). It does not supply a test procedure; that is what the red-team vectors and falsification protocol add.
- Relevant distinction: the framework's "egocentric" vs "fair-minded" critical thinking. A skilled arguer can be egocentric — effective at defending, poor at testing. Steelmanning is specifically the fair-minded corrective.

### Zenko — *Red Team: How to Succeed by Thinking Like the Enemy* (2015)

- Based on case studies across military, intelligence, and business settings.
- Zenko's central claim is narrower than popular usage: red teaming works best when it is **structured, independent, and focused on a specific plan or assumption** — not when it is a generic "second opinion."
- Emphasized elements adopted here: an independent challenger, named alternative hypotheses, explicit ranking of findings, and a requirement that the plan's owner respond.
- His caution adopted here: red teams fail when they are decorative (no authority to be heard), when they mirror the sponsoring organization's assumptions, or when "red team" becomes a label for ordinary review.

### Tetlock — adversarial collaboration and forecasting

- *Expert Political Judgment* (2005): expert accuracy is poorly predicted by eminence or ideology; it improves with explicit probabilistic reasoning and with attention to counter-arguments.
- *Superforecasting* (Tetlock & Gardner, 2015): better forecasters update in small increments, distinguish degrees of uncertainty, and actively seek disconfirming evidence.
- Adversarial collaboration (with Mellers and others, including the Kahneman–Klein reconciliation efforts): rival partisans jointly commit to a test design and to concession conditions before seeing results. Adopted here as the "what would change your mind, decided in advance" protocol.
- Tetlock's work supports the *value of stating uncertainty and pre-committing to updates* more strongly than it supports any specific steelman wording.

### Popper — falsifiability

- *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* (1959): a claim is empirical to the extent that it forbids some observable outcomes.
- Adopted here as the pre-commitment step: name the observation, threshold, and deadline. Popper's point is logical; the practical problem the skill addresses is psychological (motivated explaining-away), which is why the "rationalization trap" is written down in advance.

### Principle of charity

- Formulations in informal logic and philosophy of language, notably Rapoport's rules of argument and Dennett's *The Intentional Stance* (1987) / *Intuition Pumps* (2013) restatement: re-express the rival position so they agree, list what you agree with, note what you learned, then rebut.
- The sign-off test in `steelman.md` is a compressed version of Rapoport's first rule.

## What is established vs asserted

| Claim in this skill | Status |
|---|---|
| Strawmanning is common and inflates confidence | Well supported in argumentation research; widely observed |
| Structuring critique improves its yield over free-form skepticism | Supported by red-team case literature (Zenko), not by controlled trials of this exact protocol |
| Pre-committing to falsifiers reduces post-hoc rationalization | Supported in judgment/decision research on precommitment and in forecasting work; effect sizes vary |
| Adversarial collaboration improves dispute resolution | Demonstrated in specific scientific disputes; generalization to everyday claims is an extrapolation |
| "Steelmanning raises decision quality by X" | **Not established.** No reliable effect size; do not cite one |

## Misuse to avoid

- **Asymmetric charity.** Steelmanning the side you prefer, strawmanning the other. The procedure is only as good as its symmetry.
- **Manufactured controversy.** Steelmanning a fringe position to imply the mainstream is uncertain. Strength of the argument is not evidence of its prevalence.
- **Endless dialectic.** Reconstructing rivals forever as a substitute for deciding. The artifact requires a posterior and an action.
- **Red-team theater.** Producing attacks that are already answered in the plan, to demonstrate rigor. A real red team produces at least one hit the author did not anticipate.
- **Weaponized skepticism.** Using the "what would falsify it?" question to dismiss claims that are difficult but not impossible to test.

## Related methods in this marketplace

- `premortem` (creative track): imagines a future failure; this skill constructs the present counter-case. Complementary, not duplicative.
- `argument-mapping`: parses whether an argument's premises support its conclusion; use it to make the attack vectors precise.
- `fallacy-bias-spotting`: names the failure mode once an attack lands.
- `calibration-forecasting`: turns the pre-commitment into a scored, trackable forecast.
